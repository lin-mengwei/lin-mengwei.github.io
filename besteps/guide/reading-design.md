Most chapters of the Guide take 15 to 25 minutes to read. Around the book's text, which is unchanged, this web edition adds a few reading aids. Below is what each one does and the research that motivated it. The studies were done with other texts and readers, so take them as good reasons rather than promises.

## Read a chapter in sittings

Attention drifts the longer we read: across 68 studies, thinking about something else becomes steadily more frequent over time and is common after 20 minutes [1]. Short breaks reduce fatigue [2]. Chapters longer than 12 minutes are therefore offered in two or three sittings that end at section boundaries. "Done for today" saves your place, an optional calendar entry reminds you, and when you come back a short card lists the sections already read.

## Put each section in your own words

Recalling what you have just read is one of the most effective ways to learn [3], and it holds attention: in an online lecture, short recall questions between segments halved mind-wandering [4]. Writing a few keywords after reading also counters the shallow processing typical of screen reading [5]. The recall box at the end of each section asks for a few words of your own and keeps them with your notes. It is optional.

## See the structure, not a wall of text

Cues that show how a text is organised help readers remember it and apply it [6]. Hence the section labels with their reading time, the markers at the end of each section, the reading path in the contents, and the previews of figures and sections, which let you look something up without losing your place.

## Progress counted in sections

Progress that looks slow at the start makes people give up, while progress that looks quick keeps them going [7], and motivation tends to sag in the middle of a task [8]. Progress is therefore shown by section, where you are in the first half and how many sections are left after the middle, rather than as a countdown of minutes.

## Type that suits you

No single font is fastest for everyone: in a study of 352 adults, each reader's fastest font was about 35% faster than their slowest, and their favourite font did not predict which one it was [9]. The Aa menu offers three fonts, four text sizes, and line and letter spacing. Lines stay at about 65–70 characters.

## Light by default

For most readers in ordinary light, dark text on a light background is the easiest to read [10]. Night and Green are there for comfort and taste; they make no claim to protect your eyes (blue-light filters, for example, show no effect on eye strain [11]).

## Notes that stay with you

Explaining ideas to yourself deepens understanding [12]. The Notes panel files each note under the section you are reading, can quote a sentence from the text, and downloads everything as a Markdown file. Notes, settings and your reading position are stored only in your browser and are never sent anywhere.
